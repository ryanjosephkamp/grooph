Slice 0094 · sketches, not the product

Other ways to see a loop graph in three dimensions

The stairs you like are here as D0. Under them are four other views, each drawn from the same real documents, each live. A view is here only if it makes one true thing about a graph plain that the stairs and the flat picture do not.

Drag a picture to turn it; pinch, or the − and + beside it, to move in and out. Play walks it through a first pass, or through a run's own notes. The four buttons that stay at the top change the document for every view at once, so the same graph can be compared in all five. Answer with the labels: D1, D2, D3, D4.

D0the reference

The stairs

What the app draws today: a loop is a floor, and its members stand on it. It shows which loop a node is in. It does not show the order of a round, how many rounds there may be, or what anything costs.

Redrawn here with this page's pencil, so that all five are drawn alike: the floors, the rows of three and the arcs are the app's, the cards are plainer.

D1a loop is a ring

Rings

A round is one trip round the ring. It shows the order of a round, where the loop is entered and where it is left, and a fail that cuts across to the start instead of going all the way round: the shape of the loop itself, which a floor does not have and the flat picture leaves you to trace.

The members stand in the order a first pass meets them. An edge to the next member runs along the ring; the way back from the last member is the rest of the ring, which is what closes it; any other way back is an arch across. A loop inside a loop is a small ring standing on the large one, where its members would be.

D2what a brake looks like

The spiral and its lid

A round is one turn upward, and the brake is the ceiling. It shows how many rounds a loop may take, which of its brakes is met first, and for a run how many rounds it took, where it failed and passed on the way up, and how much room was left under the lid.

The solid lid is at the round where max iterations stops the loop. A budget counted in dispatches is a second, dashed lid at the height that many dispatches reach if every round is a full one (a dispatch is an agent or a check, as the lead counts them; a loop inside this one is counted at as many full rounds as its own lid allows). The lower of the two is the one met first when the rounds are full; a round cut short uses fewer dispatches, so the dashed lid can only be higher than drawn, never lower. A budget in turns or minutes has no height in rounds; it is in the line above, with the loop's other stops, in core's own words. The faint spiral is every round the lid allows; the solid part is the first pass, or what a run took, with a bead for each dispatch, green for a pass and amber for a fail.

D3the picture, pulled apart

Panes

The flat picture as it is, with each loop and each subgrooph lifted toward you on a pane of its own. Face on, it is the picture. Turned, it shows what is inside what, and which edges pass from one pane to another: an edge that changes depth is entering or leaving a loop.

Every node is where the canvas's own layout puts it. A node stands one pane forward for each loop or box it is in, so a loop inside a loop is two panes out, and a subgrooph's pane (dashed) is behind the loop it holds.

D4what it costs

Columns

A node is as tall as what it costs. For a template that is the tier of model it asks for, frontier above strong above fast, and nothing for a check or a gate. For a run it is one block for each dispatch, as tall as its minutes: where the strongest models are asked for, and where a run's time went.

A tier is a class of model, not a price: the heights say which is above which, not by how much. A node with no tier of its own takes the session's, and its column is drawn faint. A run's minutes are by the stamps on its notes, from the end of the dispatch before to the end of this one, so the lead's own time between two dispatches is in the later one.

If I had to pick

D2 first. Brakes are what grooph is for, and nothing it draws today shows one. On the recorded run it says in one look what the run page says in a paragraph: two rounds taken of five allowed, a fail at the critic on the first and a pass on the second. On Gauntlet it shows something I had not seen in the document until it was drawn: the outer loop's budget of 42 dispatches is two more than its four rounds can use at their fullest.

  • D1 is the one for a stranger: it is what the word "loop" looks like, and the easiest to read without a legend.
  • D3 is the nearest to the flat picture, so the picture would become it with the least travel.
  • D4 earns its place on a run's page and is thin on a template.

Every picture on this page is drawn from four documents in the repository: the templates review-gate and gauntlet-decomposed, the fixture subgrooph-in-a-graph, and the recorded run of slice 0007. The rows, the stops' words, the roles and the run's steps are worked out by grooph's own core when the page is built; the page fetches nothing. Each view is a few dozen lines that place the graph in space, on one small stage of about two hundred that draws on a 2D canvas, with no library: any of them can go into the app behind the door the 3D view is behind now.
