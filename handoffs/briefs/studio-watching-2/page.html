Slice 0109, round two · sketches, not the product

More ways to draw a watched session

The first page had six drawings, W1 to W6. This one has seven more: three of the six again in three dimensions, where depth says something the flat one cannot, and four new ideas, W7 to W10. Same three sessions, same clock.

Play steps a session through every moment at which something changed. All seven keep the same moment. Touch anything in a drawing to read what the record has of it. Under each drawing is the same drawing at the large session's last moment, at a phone's width and without the made-up statements: the end, as the one picture an export would hold.

Amber is the place kept for what a session says it will do. No session said anything here: the statements are made up, each drawing says so under itself, and Said, at the top, takes them away.

  • green: running
  • plain: finished
  • red cross: failed
  • Opus, Sonnet, Haiku
  • dashed tie: started in the background
  • amber, dashed: said, never ran (made up here)
  • amber diamond: ran, never said (made up here)

In three dimensions

Three of the first six gain something from depth, and are drawn below. The other three do not, and here is why.

  • W1 in three dimensions is W5. Stand the tree's tiles on a floor in the order of starting and give each its time as height, and that is the skyline; a tree with only generations for height would have two steps and nothing on the second but two agents.
  • W3 already spends its second dimension on what a third would carry: a line's height over the trunk is how many were out at once, and the rows are the fold. Wound into a spiral it would lose its stations behind its own arches.
  • W5 is in three dimensions already. What it lacks is turning under a thumb, which is the app's stage's to give it and not a new drawing.

These three are drawn at one fixed angle with this page's pencil. In the app the helix and the floors could stand on the stage that four of its views in three dimensions share (panes, spiral, rings, columns), which turns under a finger; the road needs a view of its own.

W2 3Dtime as depth

The road

W2's lanes, with time running away from you. The near edge is the present; everything that happened runs back from it toward the session's first line. Far things are small, so the whole session is in one view with the present large. A seam across the road is where a burst began.

  • Good at: the one thing the flat lanes cannot do: show now in detail and the whole past at once. Five at once is five blocks side by side at your feet; yesterday is a few thin strips near the horizon.
  • Where it breaks: the far past is too small to touch or to name, and a short run far back is a sliver. It draws depth by burst and not by the clock, so two days of waiting are one seam.
  • To build: it needs a projection of its own: the app's stage for three dimensions makes far things only a little smaller, by design, and the road is all foreshortening. The lanes are W2's. About 2.5 KB compressed for the drawing itself as drawn here, fetched only when chosen.
  • To export: the picture of the end is mostly the last burst, with the session small behind it, so it is better as a replay than as a still; the replay is a page that plays; a video reads well at a phone's size, since the eye stays at the near edge.

W4 3Dthe ring, wound upward

The helix

W4's rim wound into a rising coil. Every subagent has one place on it, in the order of starting, the oldest at the foot; a mark on the mast is a day gone by. What is running stands at the top beside the session, with a thread down to its own place.

  • Good at: giving the past an order and a height, which the flat ring keeps only as a count of beads. A day with many starts is a long stretch of coil between two marks; the present is still the first thing the eye meets.
  • Where it breaks: the half of the coil behind the mast is fainter and harder to touch, and durations are still nowhere. On a live session the coil has to be spaced again as it grows.
  • To build: small: W4 with a rise, about 2.5 KB compressed for the drawing itself as drawn here. The app's spiral is the nearest piece it has, and is a different thing: a round of a loop a turn.
  • To export: the picture of the end is the whole coil, and a handsome one; the replay plays well; a video reads at a phone's size, the same as W4's.

W6 3Da burst a floor

Floors

W6's bursts as the floors of one building, the newest on top. Every subagent has one place on the plan, the same on every floor, so one that is sent back out stands over where it stood, and a thread runs up through its place from floor to floor.

  • Good at: who came back, and when: a thread through three floors is an agent resumed twice. The flat sentence of shapes shows each burst and loses that one agent is in several of them.
  • Where it breaks: only the newest six floors are drawn, so it is a view of the recent past and not of the session; and a floor hides part of the one under it.
  • To build: the nearest piece the app has is its panes, where a loop is lifted on a pane; a floor a burst, one place for each agent on every floor and the threads between them are new. About 2.5 KB compressed for the drawing itself as drawn here, fetched only when chosen.
  • To export: the picture of the end holds six floors and says how many are under them, so it is the weakest of these as an export; the replay is where it works; a video reads, at a phone's size, for the threads more than for the names.

Four new ideas

None of these is one of the six again. Two are tables and charts more than graphs, and are here because the end of a session, as one picture, is what an export has to be.

W7a row an agent, a column a burst

The punch card

A row for each subagent, in the order of starting; a column for each burst of work; a mark where that subagent ran in that burst. New agents make the diagonal. An agent sent back out is a second mark along its row. A tall column is a burst that fanned out.

  • Good at: the whole history of a session of days in a few hundred pixels, with nothing folded and nothing hidden: who exists, when each worked, who came back and how often. Its end is the best single picture of a long session on this page.
  • Where it breaks: who started whom is only a small tie at the margin, how long a run took is not there at all, and a short session is one column. At fifty-eight rows a mark is three pixels tall.
  • To build: the least of the round: rectangles on a grid, in the themes' colors, about 2 KB compressed for the drawing itself as drawn here. Light enough to ride with the live screen.
  • To export: the picture of the end is the whole record at a glance, and it is the drawing whose data is nearest its picture (a table of agents by bursts); the replay fills along the diagonal; a video reads at a phone's size but says little a still does not.

W8who ran beside whom

Arcs

Every subagent is a point on one line, in the order of starting, and an arc joins two that ran at the same time. A fan of five is a knot of ten arcs. An agent that worked alone is a bare point. One sent back out beside newer agents throws a long arc back to them. Under the line, an arc from a subagent to one it started.

  • Good at: the shape of the fan-outs: how often the session ran agents together, how many, and which agents were ever at work at once. No other drawing here answers that directly.
  • Where it breaks: it says nothing of when or for how long, and the session itself is only the line. With dozens of agents on a phone the line folds in two, and a pair that ran together across the fold is two short arms and not an arc.
  • To build: small: points and arcs, about 2 KB compressed for the drawing itself as drawn here, in the themes' colors.
  • To export: the picture of the end is the prettiest on the page and exports cleanly; the replay adds arcs as agents overlap; a video reads at a phone's size.

W9a ring a burst

Growth rings

The session as the cross-section of a tree. A ring for each burst of work, the first at the heart, thicker the more work was in it. Round a ring, an arc for each subagent that ran in that burst, as long as its share of the burst's time. The outer ring is the one that is growing.

  • Good at: where the work went, burst by burst and model by model, in one round picture: a thick ring was a heavy burst, and its colors say on which models.
  • Where it breaks: who started whom is not in it, nor background and foreground, and thin rings near the heart cannot be touched. Live, a ring's arcs can only be shares so far, since nobody knows how long a burst will be.
  • To build: small to middling: sectors of rings, about 2 KB compressed for the drawing itself as drawn here. Nothing in the app's flat pictures is round yet.
  • To export: the picture of the end is a poster: the whole session as one disc; the replay grows it outward; a video reads at a phone's size.

W10how many at once

The pulse

The plainest of them: how many were running at once, moment by moment, stacked by model. A step is a moment, so days of waiting cost nothing. Under the line, a tick where each subagent first started, and a mark where a day has gone by.

  • Good at: being read in a glance, by anyone, with nothing to learn. It would also be the natural handle for a replay: drag along it, and every other drawing would follow. On this page it is not that yet; the bar under it is.
  • Where it breaks: it is a chart and not a graph: no agent can be followed through it, and who started whom is not there.
  • To build: the simplest there is to draw: a stepped area, about 2.5 KB compressed for the drawing itself as drawn here. It could ride with the live screen and sit over any other drawing as its clock.
  • To export: the picture of the end is a plain chart that anyone can read; in a replay it would be the bar a person drags; a video of it reads better at a phone's size than any other here.

What exporting each would mean

Three things can be taken out of any of them. The data is the same for all thirteen: the graph at the last moment with its list of moments, as slice 0104's function gives it, as JSON; a drawing is a view of it. The picture of the end is the drawing at the last moment as an image, as a loop graph's picture is today. The replay is a page that plays, as these two pages do, and from it a video. For the seven on this page the line is under each drawing. For the first six:

  • W1 The picture of the end is the whole tree, every agent in its place: it exports as it stands. Nothing moves in the replay, so a video reads at a phone's size.
  • W2 The end is only the last burst. An export would be the strip with one burst chosen, or every burst on a long sheet; a video jumps from burst to burst and is hard to follow.
  • W3 The picture of the end is the whole map, and the best single picture among the six. The replay draws it row by row; a video reads.
  • W4 The end is a ring of beads and says little: this one is for the present. A video is its natural export, and reads at a phone's size.
  • W5 The end is the skyline, a good picture with no names on it. The replay grows it; a video reads, without the names.
  • W6 The end is the sentence of shapes with the last burst open. The replay reads well; a video reads for the sentence and not for the cards.

If I had to pick

Of this round: W10 first, then W7, then W2 3D. The pulse is the simplest to build and could do a second job nothing else here can: be the handle of a replay, the bar a person drags while any other drawing follows. The punch card is the best single picture of a long session, which is what an export has to be, and its data is nearest its picture. The road is the three-dimensional one that earns its depth: it is the only drawing on either page that shows the present in detail and the whole past at once.

With the first six, in this order.

  • 1. W1 with W10 over it. The tree as the live graph, the pulse as its clock and its replay bar. W6's idea, a burst at a time, is how W1 stays readable on a long session.
  • 2. W3 and W7, the two that hold a whole session in one picture: the line map for order and who came back to whom, the punch card for who worked when. These are the export pictures.
  • 3. W4, then W4 3D where a glance is all there is. The helix is W4 with a rise, so it is cheap once the ring is there.
  • 4. W2, then W2 3D. Time and overlap, flat for one burst and in depth for the whole.
  • 5. W5 and W9, the two posters: where the time went, as a city and as a disc.
  • 6. W8 and W6 3D last. Arcs is the prettiest and answers the question asked least. Floors shows six bursts and hides the rest.

Every drawing on this page is drawn from the same three records as the first page, and the page fetches nothing. Four at once is the watched run the repository holds; the other two are real sessions of this project, kept as a shape and nothing else, with the gaps between their bursts as they were. Every id is renumbered and every time is counted from the session's first line. Nothing a session read, wrote or said is here. The amber marks stand where a session's own statements would go; every statement on this page is made up, and each drawing says so under itself. A burst is this page's own reading (runs with less than ten minutes between them). The figures for weight are each drawing's own script as written for this page; all of them also share about 9 KB of script compressed, and a stylesheet.
