# The reference statement, and what matters about it

`reference.txt` beside this file is the statement the billing team already
sends, for the same customer and month as the project's data. The critic
compares each capture against it. What matters, most first:

1. **The total is found at once.** The figures end with the total due, set off
   by a rule, its amount in the amount column. Above it, in this order: the
   charges, a line that adds them up ("Charges"), then the credits.
2. **Largest first.** Charges are listed by amount, largest first, and two
   charges of the same amount by name. Credits are listed the same way, the
   largest credit first. The reader sees where the money went without
   searching.
3. **Amounts read as money.** Dollars and cents with a comma between
   thousands, set so the decimal points fall in one column down the whole
   statement. The currency is named once, in the column heading, and never as
   a sign on an amount. A credit is written in parentheses, not with a minus
   sign, and its digits still fall in line with the amounts above it.
4. **Nothing that says nothing.** A service with no usage is not a line of the
   table. One sentence at the foot says how many were left out and names them.
5. **Each charge's share.** A column gives each charge as a percentage of the
   charges, to one decimal.
6. **The month by name.** "September 2026", never "2026-09". The customer's
   name opens the statement, with the account number at the right of the same
   line.
7. **Usage with its unit.** The quantity, with a comma between thousands, and
   its unit, read together: "18,250 vCPU-hours".

A **major** gap is one a customer notices at a glance, or one that could
mislead: charges in an order other than by amount; no line adding up the
charges, or the credits mixed in among them; decimal points that do not line
up; thousands without a comma; a minus sign or a currency sign on an amount; a
service with no usage listed as a line; the month in digits; no share column.
The exact column widths, the wording of the headings, and the length of the
rules are not gaps at all unless they break one of the seven points above.
