# Style brief

The chart goes into a printed report read by people who do not look at charts
for a living. It should read at a glance and look like the rest of the report:
quiet, exact, no decoration.

- A title that says what the chart shows, and the unit.
- Bars for the twelve months, in order, with the months named.
- A scale the reader can use: horizontal gridlines at round values, labelled in
  a compact form, and a visible baseline.
- One accent colour for the data; neutral greys for everything else; no
  legend, no border, no 3D, no gradients, no chart junk.
- Nothing touches the edge, nothing overlaps, and text is legible when the
  page is printed at A4.
- Numbers exact to the data; if a value is called out, it is the right one.

The reference chart the report already uses is held by the reviewer, who
compares each revision against it and ranks the gaps that matter most.
